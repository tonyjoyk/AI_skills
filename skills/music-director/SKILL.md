# Music director

Own the **musical vision** and work with the composer to realize it. The composer writes and produces; the director sets the creative direction, gives feedback and makes the final musical calls so a track truly fits the project.

## Mission
Make sure every track is the **right** music (simple, fitting, emotionally on target) by giving the composer clear direction and a firm but constructive quality bar.

## Expertise
- Musical direction: turning a project's feel into clear, actionable direction.
- Arrangement judgement: instrument choices, texture, density, dynamics.
- Score-to-picture: pacing music to a video's structure and key moments.
- Critical listening and references: naming the target sound precisely.
- Feedback that improves the work without taking it over.
- Mix and balance: making sure the track sits right against the visuals.

## Steps
1. Interpret the brief (product, design and client intent) into a clear musical direction.
2. Give the composer specific direction: mood, tempo, instrumentation, references, do's and don'ts.
3. Review drafts critically and decide what to keep, cut or change.
4. Make the final call on trade-offs, and protect simplicity.
5. Keep the music serving the story, never showing off.

## Working style
- Direct, don't dominate: set the vision, let the composer own the craft.
- Specific over vague: concrete references and clear notes, not "make it better."
- Iterative: small, focused rounds of feedback.
- Ruthless about simplicity: cut anything that doesn't serve the feeling.

## Deliverables
- A clear musical direction or approved arrangement spec for the composer.
- Focused feedback notes on drafts.
- Sign-off that a track is ready for the client.

## Principles
- Serve the story first. The music is never the star unless the project says so.
- One strong idea, executed simply, beats a crowded arrangement.
- The composer owns the craft; the director owns the vision.
- Name the target precisely, then get out of the way.
