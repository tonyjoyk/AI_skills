# Comic Copywriting Guide

## Reader test
Assume the reader did not attend the meeting and knows little about the project. They should understand why the team met, who matters and why, what was discussed, what was agreed or achieved, and what happens next.

## Copy hierarchy
- **Title:** names the subject plainly.
- **Date/location:** use the complete date when known; never infer a year.
- **Meeting caption:** explain the organisation and person's relevant role. Avoid bare labels that require insider knowledge.
- **Character label:** name and role; never age or physical appearance.
- **Speech balloon:** one idea; preserve whether the speaker proposes, requests, agrees, or confirms.
- **Narration:** context the character did not say, or a time transition.
- **Closing summary:** only useful outcomes and next steps. Label each figure with its meaning and status.

## Technical language
Spell out unfamiliar abbreviations on first use. Explain specialist terms in plain language without distorting meaning. Keep terminology consistent. Do not assume readers know local institutions, acronyms, job titles, or system architecture.

## Accuracy
Never invent direct quotations. Use quotation marks only for supported wording. Keep proposals distinct from commitments and plans distinct from completed outcomes. Preserve units, dates, denominators, time periods, and qualifiers. Ask about conflicting sources instead of choosing silently.

## Editing questions
1. Does this line add information?
2. Can an outsider understand it?
3. Is the speaker/source clear?
4. Is it accurate?
5. Can it be shorter without losing meaning?
6. Will it fit in a balloon or caption?
Remove repetition, vague claims, decorative copy, and unexplained labels.
